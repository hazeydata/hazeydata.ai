What is the Wait Time Index (WTI)?

Think of WTI as the "temperature" of a theme park — a single number that tells you how busy a park is on any given day.

Planning a theme park visit shouldn't feel like solving a puzzle. You want to know one simple thing: how busy will the park be? That's exactly what the Wait Time Index (WTI) tells you.

WTI 23

One number. The complete picture of a park's crowds.

The Temperature Analogy

Just like you check the weather forecast before getting dressed, WTI lets you check the "crowd forecast" before visiting a park. Think of it this way:

🌡️ WTI is like temperature for theme parks

Low WTI (15-20) = Cool, comfortable day with short waits
High WTI (30+) = Hot, crowded day with long lines everywhere

You wouldn't plan an outdoor barbecue without checking if it's 70°F or 95°F. Similarly, you shouldn't plan a park day without knowing if it's a WTI 18 or WTI 32.

How WTI Works

The Wait Time Index distills all the complexity of park crowds into a single, easy-to-understand number. Here's the magic behind it:

The Calculation (High Level)

WTI represents the average predicted actual wait time across all operating attractions in a park on a given day. Every ride counts equally — no weighting, no secret sauce. It's a straightforward average that gives you an honest picture of what your day will feel like.

🧮 The Formula

WTI = average of all predicted actual wait times across every operating attraction in the park for that day. Simple, transparent, and meaningful.

The key word there is "actual" wait times — not the posted times you see on the app. Our models predict what you'll really wait, which is often different from what's posted on the board. That prediction accuracy is what makes WTI reliable.

A Different Way to Think About Crowds

If you've planned a theme park trip, you've probably used a crowd calendar — and you should! Tools like TouringPlans' 1-to-10 scale are genuinely valuable for trip planning. WTI isn't here to replace them. It's a different lens on the same problem.

What Makes WTI Different

Traditional crowd calendars distill crowds into a relative scale (like 1 to 10). That's great for comparing days at a glance. WTI takes a different approach — it gives you a number in real units you can feel.

15+

Years of wait time data powering every prediction

When WTI says "23," that maps directly to roughly 23 minutes of average wait time. You can immediately picture what your day looks like — how long the lines will be, whether you need to rope drop, how many rides you'll realistically fit in.

Use Both

Honestly? The best approach is to use multiple tools. Check a crowd calendar for the big-picture view, then use WTI to understand what those crowd levels actually feel like in terms of wait times. More information is always better when you're planning a trip.

How to Use WTI

Using WTI to plan your perfect park day is straightforward:

The Golden Rules

🎯 WTI Planning Strategy

Low WTI (15-22): Great days! You can be flexible, arrive later, and still experience everything.

High WTI (28+): Rope drop is essential. Have a plan. Take advantage of priority queues like Lightning Lane or Universal Express.

The beauty of WTI is its simplicity. Instead of juggling multiple crowd factors, you have one clear number to guide your decisions.

Park-Specific Context Matters

Here's where WTI gets really smart: a WTI of 25 doesn't feel the same at every park. The system accounts for each park's unique characteristics:

Magic Kingdom vs. Animal Kingdom

Magic Kingdom has 25+ major attractions spread across a large area. Animal Kingdom has fewer rides but includes long shows and experiences. A WTI of 25 at Magic Kingdom might feel manageable — plenty of options to spread crowds. The same WTI at Animal Kingdom could feel more intense because you have fewer alternatives.

🏰 Park Context Example

Magic Kingdom WTI 25: Moderate day, but you have 25+ attractions to choose from
Animal Kingdom WTI 25: Same numerical score, but with only 8 major rides, it feels busier

WTI accounts for these differences in its calculations, but understanding the context helps you plan better. That's why we provide park-specific insights alongside the raw numbers.

The Science Behind the Scenes

WTI isn't just a number — it's the output of machine learning models (XGBoost) trained on over 15 years of real wait time data. The system considers:

  • Historical wait patterns for each attraction across thousands of days
  • Seasonal trends and time-of-year patterns
  • Day-of-week effects
  • Park operating schedules and ride closures
  • The relationship between posted and actual wait times (they're often very different!)

Every morning, the pipeline runs fresh predictions and WTI scores are updated automatically. No manual guessing — just data and models.
