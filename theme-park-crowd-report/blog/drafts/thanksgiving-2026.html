Thanksgiving 2026 at Disney World: Wait Times Are Dropping — But It's Still Packed

Here's a fun fact: Thanksgiving week standby wait times at Disney World have dropped in three out of the last three years. At Magic Kingdom, the average WTI fell from 18.6 in 2023 to 14.5 in 2025. That's a real, measurable decline — not a blip.

So does that make Thanksgiving 2026 the year to finally pull the trigger on a holiday trip?

The honest answer: yes and no.

Yes — our 2026 forecast suggests wait times will remain in that lower range, making it one of the more manageable Thanksgiving weeks in recent memory. No — because even a "lighter" Thanksgiving week is still one of the top three or four busiest weeks of the entire year. You will encounter large crowds. You will wait in lines. The question is how much, and whether you can plan around the worst of it.

Let's dig into the numbers.

What Is the Wait Time Index (WTI)?

Quick primer if you're new here. WTI is a single number — the average actual standby wait time across every operating attraction at a given park. Not just the headliners. Not a subjective "crowd level." One number, no asterisks.

Here's how we think about it:

  • WTI under 15 — Light crowds. Walk-on territory for most rides.
  • WTI 15–22 — Moderate. Very doable with minimal planning.
  • WTI 22–30 — Busy. You'll feel it, especially at headliners.
  • WTI 30+ — Peak crowds. Budget extra time for everything.

During Thanksgiving week, WTI across the four Disney World parks has historically ranged from the mid-teens to the low 30s, depending on the park and the day. That's a big spread — and it's where smart planning makes all the difference.

Let's start with the headline numbers. Here's how Thanksgiving week (the full Mon–Sun window around the holiday) has looked at each park:

EPCOT could genuinely be a great park choice over the holiday.

Animal Kingdom is the wildcard (pun intended). It spiked hard in 2025 — averaging 27.4, the highest Thanksgiving WTI of any park that year. But our 2026 model shows a dramatic correction to 15.6. If that holds, AK could be the lowest-crowd option for the entire holiday week. Keep in mind: AK's shorter operating hours and fewer attractions make it more volatile day-to-day.

Thanksgiving vs. Christmas: How Does It Compare?

People always ask: is Thanksgiving as bad as Christmas? Short answer: no. But it's closer than you'd think.

For context, here's what Christmas week 2024 looked like:

  • Magic Kingdom: avg WTI 20.1
  • Hollywood Studios: avg WTI 30.1
  • Animal Kingdom: avg WTI 28.2

Compare those to Thanksgiving 2024 (MK 16.0, HS 25.6, AK 22.6) and you can see the gap — Christmas runs about 20–25% hotter across the board. So yes, Thanksgiving is genuinely lighter than Christmas. But "lighter than Christmas" and "light" are two very different things. You're still dealing with holiday-level crowds.

Day-by-Day: When Thanksgiving Week Gets Ugly (and When It Doesn't)

Not all days during Thanksgiving week are created equal. Here's the pattern we've seen consistently across multiple years:

  • Monday & Tuesday (Nov 23–24): The busiest days, especially at Hollywood Studios and Animal Kingdom. Families arriving for the week hit the parks right away. HS has topped WTI 30 on these days in previous years.
  • Wednesday (Nov 25): Variable. Some years it's a continuation of the Monday/Tuesday crush; other years it dips as people settle in or travel. Don't count on it being lighter.
  • Thanksgiving Day (Nov 26): Surprisingly moderate. A good chunk of families are at their resort or dining, which thins park crowds. This is often one of the best days of the week for actual wait times.
  • Friday (Nov 27): Moderate to heavy. The "we skipped the parks yesterday, let's go today" crowd shows up. Still generally better than Monday/Tuesday.
  • Saturday & Sunday (Nov 28–29): Crowds lighten as families begin heading home. These are often the most pleasant days of the week — especially Sunday.

The takeaway: If you can shift your heavy park days to Thursday, Saturday, or Sunday — and use Monday/Tuesday for resort days, Disney Springs, or water parks — you'll dodge the worst of it.

So Should You Go to Disney World for Thanksgiving 2026?

Let's be real about what the data is telling us:

The case for going: Wait times are genuinely trending lower. Our 2026 forecasts show every park coming in lighter than recent years — and in some cases (EPCOT, Animal Kingdom) significantly lighter. If you've been waiting for Thanksgiving crowds to ease up, this is the best outlook we've had in years. Plus, the holiday decorations, the food festivals, and the cooler Florida weather make it a legitimately special time to visit.

The case for caution: Even at these lower levels, Thanksgiving week is still one of the busiest weeks of the year. A "light" Thanksgiving at Hollywood Studios (WTI 21.9) is still busier than an average week in September or October. You're not going to walk onto Rise of the Resistance. You're still going to encounter crowds at rope drop, long restaurant waits, and packed buses.

Our take: go — but go prepared. Use the data. Know which days are heavier. Check the forecast the morning of each park day. Have a backup plan. The people who have a miserable time during Thanksgiving week are almost always the ones who didn't plan for what "busy but improving" actually looks like in practice.

Quick Tips for Thanksgiving Week 2026

  • Front-load rest, back-load parks. Arrive Saturday or Sunday before Thanksgiving. Use Mon–Tue for rest days. Hit the parks Wed–Sun when crowds are lighter.
  • Hollywood Studios early in the week, if you must. HS crowds peak early in the week. If it's your must-do park, tackle it Monday with a rope drop strategy and Lightning Lane.
  • Thanksgiving Day is underrated. Seriously — go to a park on Thursday. Crowds have historically been moderate, and you can still do a nice dinner after.
  • EPCOT and AK could be your best bets. Both are forecasting significantly lower WTI than HS or MK. If you're flexible on which parks to visit, lean toward these two.
  • Check forecasts daily. Drop into our Discord and run /crowd each morning. Day-to-day swings of 5+ WTI points are common during holiday weeks, and catching a lighter day can transform your experience.

The Bottom Line

Thanksgiving 2026 at Disney World is shaping up to be the lightest holiday week in recent memory — but "lightest" is relative. The downward trend in wait times is real and encouraging. EPCOT and Animal Kingdom in particular are forecasting WTI numbers that would have been unthinkable a few years ago during a holiday week.

But don't confuse "lighter than 2023" with "light." This is still a top-tier busy week. Plan accordingly, stay flexible, and let the data guide your day-to-day decisions.
